October 26, 2026.

**Map refinement:** the [parcel maps](third-ward-maps-and-listed-assemblages.md)
separate Third Ward proper from grid spillovers. The Drew/Tuam square's 60
candidate records contain 45 on the Third Ward side and 15 surrounding sites.
The polygon audit narrows the default side-by-side view to **27 Drew/Tuam and
24 Rosedale/Arbor sites**. The other 24 source sites are in a separate layout view.
Use those audited pools for the current comparison. The eight-square inventory
below retains its original operational-grid scope and older frontage/fill screen.

**Third Ward near UH/TSU belongs in the first acquisition wave.** The initial
shortlist selected concentrated campaigns reaching resident individual owners.
Adding quality scores only to those five leading campaign pockets did not answer
which locations best suit the buyer's project. It left Third Ward out before its
commute advantages, assemblies and smaller owner audiences were compared.

Keep two acquisition lanes: concentrated geographic campaigns where resident
target owners are numerous, and property-specific sourcing in Third Ward near
UH/TSU. Owner residence determines how to reach a seller; it does not determine
whether the buyer wants the land. Business ownership also remains eligible.

## Calibrate the block screen against Drew

The known 3902/3906 Drew project triggers our numeric repeat-violence review.
Using only the **April 24, 2025–April 23, 2026** period before the first reported
purchase gives **7 distinct serious incidents within 660 ft, across 6 months,
including 3 in public settings**: six aggravated assaults and one robbery.
The standard July 2025–June 2026 comparison window produces eight incidents.

The buyer's reported purchase establishes that this threshold cannot be an
automatic rejection rule. We do not know whether the buyer considered these
incident records. Retain the evidence, inspect the actual site and approach,
and record buyer decisions. A flag changes the inspection task; it does not
delete acquisition inventory or disqualify Third Ward.

For comparison, the Rosedale individual-site pool has a median of 1.5 incidents
within 660 ft and no repeat-review flags. Other Third Ward pools have materially
higher counts. Those differences justify choosing blocks carefully inside the
search area. The single individual site in the Holman/Truxillo review pool cannot
stand in for the other 24 candidate sites there.

## Acquisition price is the harder problem

Third Ward's stronger access comes with higher land expectations. The median
HCAD land assessment in the Rosedale individual review pool is $19.80/sq ft;
in the Drew pool it is $31.20. Drew itself is assessed at $33.20/sq ft. These
figures describe appraisal context, not what the buyer or a motivated seller paid.

The retrieved HAR 77004 listing snapshot shows 3703 Drew at $700,000 for 15,000 sq ft,
approximately $46.67/sq ft, and 2905 Elgin at $940,000 for 20,000 sq ft, or
$47/sq ft. Those retail asks do not support our exit. This is a reason to hunt
withdrawn/expired listings, quiet owner sales and stalled projects here.

Redfin's 77004 residential market page, covering the three months ending September
2026, reports 81 median days on
market, 33.5% of listings with price reductions and a 96.5% sale/list ratio.
Those ZIP-wide conditions support looking for unsuccessful sellers; they do not
measure qualifying land inventory.

Sources: [HAR 77004 land listings](https://www.har.com/lots-and-land-for-sale-77004?cid=GWW&include_acreage=1),

## Ranking and implementation correction

- The original five 0–100 scores remain a historical comparison of selected
  campaign pockets. They are not a ranking of all inside-Loop acquisition opportunities.
- Keep **buyer/location fit**, **campaign reach**, and **deal economics** as distinct
  decisions. A small audience or high assessment must not remove an otherwise
  useful location before its opportunities are examined.
- Crime recurrence remains explicit evidence. The existing point deductions have
  not been calibrated against buyer decisions; do not interpret their scores as
  purchase probabilities or use the flag as a buyer-eligibility gate.
- The Third Ward queue contains all 312 records, with their existing account IDs,
  layout routes, ownership categories, transit and block evidence. No source sites
  were removed. The 88 individual/estate/trust sites are an outreach subset.
